# Visual Infographic

Stage two of two. Renders the blueprint from VisualBlueprint. **The blueprint is
the authoritative source** — reproduce its content, never extend it.

## The ideal state

A page that looks hand-drawn on bright white paper by a good teacher: calm,
uncrowded, visually carried, and readable at a glance. Every mark means
something. Nothing on it was invented at render time.

## Fidelity to the blueprint

Preserve exactly: the central topic, the central visual concept, all 6-8
modules, module titles, compact facts, fragments, labels, vocabulary, marked
key words, examples, relationships, sequences, comparisons, and every span
marked `==like this==`.

Never expand a fragment into a sentence. Never invent facts, categories,
modules, examples, conclusions or interpretations. Never repeat information
already in another module. Never add sections called "Why It Matters", "Key
Points", "Summary", "Recap", "Conclusion" or "Takeaways". If the blueprint
omits something, its absence is the instruction.

## Composition

Central visual in the middle, directly below or beside the title box, as the
one larger drawing. Modules arranged around it in clear reading order —
**unnumbered**, at slightly different heights and widths, never on a strict
grid. Generous white space between modules is the style, not waste.

**Title box only:** a simple hand-drawn rectangle, clean black outline, one
shaded side and bottom edge in orange for a slight flat 3D lift. White inside,
title hand-lettered in all-caps over two to four lines. No gradients, glows,
rendered shadows or coloured top faces. This treatment is reserved for the title.

Each module carries: an unnumbered all-caps handwritten header; 1-3 simple
topic-specific doodles; the most suitable diagram, chart or sub-panel when
useful; concise labels and precise annotations; the blueprint's marked spans
highlighted in place; and a meaningful visual connection to the centre.

## Separators — the rule that keeps it from looking digital

Modules are **never** inside rigid frames, cards, panels or complete boxes.
Separate with a varied mix: generous white space, headers, arrows, and loose
hand-drawn marks — a short underline, partial box corners, a wavy or cloud-like
line, a rough L-shape, a dotted divider, or nothing at all. Irregular, slightly
wobbly, incomplete, open. Never let a separator enclose a whole module.

No rounded cards, UI panels, sticker frames, coloured section backgrounds or
polished rectangular outlines. The only fully enclosed shapes on the page are
the title box, speech and thought bubbles, and a small plain table when the
information is genuinely tabular.

## Drawing style

Simple line cartoons, the way a teacher sketches on a whiteboard. People with
round white heads, **visible simple eyes**, a curved mouth where appropriate,
thin straight limbs. Every human figure needs at least one readable identifier
drawn from that person's role, occupation, setting, activity or relationship to
the topic — a prop, garment, hairstyle, posture, tool or companion. Vary them;
never repeat the same generic person.

Objects as recognisable outlines with one or two identifying details. Line
black, thin, slightly wobbly rather than polished. Avoid stock illustration,
realistic anatomy, detailed faces, complex texture, elaborate perspective,
glossy surfaces, commercial pictograms and finished editorial-cartoon design.

## Connectors

Purposeful hand-drawn arrows, leader lines, dotted lines, brackets, pointer
marks and circles forming a readable knowledge network. Curved, dashed or solid
arrows link centre to modules and carry the blueprint's sequences and
relationships. **Every connector must communicate meaning** — no decorative
lines, no numbered module markers, no unnecessary cross-connections.

## Colour

Restrained. Black or dark charcoal ink carries all outlines, lettering and
connectors; most of the page stays black on white. Teal and orange for
occasional flat fills, highlighter strokes and the title box's shaded edge.
Muted red and yellow only where content calls for a warning, top level or
contrast. Never colour every icon; never fill large areas.

## Highlighting

Reproduce each compact bullet, label, date, number or fragment exactly, then
highlight **only** the `==marked==` spans. One continuous marker stroke per
span. Do not draw the equals marks. Keep the span embedded in its original
text — never extract it, repeat it standalone, or highlight unmarked text. Use
a slightly off-register translucent teal, orange, yellow or muted-red stroke
behind the letters. Not a box, pill, tag or selection bar.

## Paper and finish

Pristine bright white. No ruled lines, grid, beige tint, cream tone, parchment
texture or grey cast. Everything flat and hand-drawn. No paper-cut layers,
rendered shadows, foreshortening, gradients, glossy lighting, logos, branding,
watermarks or unrelated decoration. Never dull, faded, pastel, corporate,
sterile or clip-art.

## Lettering

Clear legible English; reproduce all supplied titles, labels, vocabulary, dates,
measurements, equations and annotations accurately. Distinct all-caps
handwritten printing for major headers, minimal handwritten lettering elsewhere.
No explanatory notes beneath drawings unless the blueprint supplies them.
Genuinely tabular information gets a small plain two-column table with thin
hand-drawn rules. Speech or thought bubbles only for a quotation the blueprint
supplies. No fake text, pseudo-writing, invented labels or irrelevant words.

## Aspect ratio

Choose from `3:4`, `1:1`, `9:16`, `16:9` to suit the module count and reading
order. Ask if it matters to the destination.

## Gotchas

- **Boxes are the failure mode.** The single biggest tell of a generated
  infographic is every module sitting in its own rounded card. Open, irregular
  separation is what makes this style work.
- **Eyes.** Figures without eyes read as corporate pictograms, not sketchnotes.
- **White space is content.** A crowded page defeats the purpose even when every
  fact on it is correct.

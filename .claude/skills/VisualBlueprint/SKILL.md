# Visual Blueprint

Stage one of two. Produces the authoritative content plan that VisualInfographic
renders. Never write the render prompt here, and never produce the image.

## The ideal state

Someone reading the blueprint knows exactly what will appear on the page and
where, in words compact enough to survive being hand-lettered. The plan carries
real knowledge, not headings waiting to be filled.

## Required inputs

- **Topic** — one specific subject
- **Learning level** — who is reading, and what they already know

Ask for the level if it is not given. A board director and a graduate engineer
need different blueprints for the same subject.

## What to produce

First identify the **subject type**: scientific concept, person, historical
event, object, place, process, system, plan, or classroom concept. The type
drives which modules make sense.

Then select **6-8 topic-specific knowledge modules**. Choose them from what the
subject actually needs — never force a fixed set. Candidates: definition,
identity, key features, parts, structure, composition, origin, background,
development, chronology, location, context, function, mechanism, process,
stages, types, classification, comparison, examples, evidence, relationships,
applications, effects, misconceptions, practical use.

For each module:

- a short module title
- one short concept label — a label, not a sentence
- **3-5 essential facts**, varying by module. Do not pad to a fixed count
- write the **exact compact text** that will appear: bullets, fragments, labels,
  names, dates, numbers, brief contrasts. One line each, usually 3-10 words
- the single most useful example, comparison, date or measurement — omit it if
  it would clutter
- the best visual representation for that module
- the relationship between the module and the central topic

Also specify **the best central visual representation** of the whole topic.

## Marking key spans

Wrap 1-2 spans per text item in `==double equals==`. A span is one word, or a
two-to-three word phrase when the meaning only holds as a unit — a named
technique, a paired term, a range, a measurement with its unit, a short
contrast. Mark the shortest span carrying the key meaning. Never mark more than
about a quarter of an item.

Marked spans stay embedded in their text. Never collect them into a glossary or
key-terms list.

## Constraints

- Level-appropriate and specific; prioritise what the reader genuinely needs
- No generic statements, no motivational content, no "why it matters" module
- **Never repeat a fact across modules.** If an idea fits two, put it in the one
  where it explains best and refer to it elsewhere only as a relationship
- Return only the blueprint. Do not write the render prompt, do not generate art

## Gotchas

- **Fragments, not sentences.** "40% utilisation, headroom to 65%" survives
  hand-lettering; a full sentence does not.
- **Module count is a range for a reason.** Six strong modules beat eight where
  two are padding.
- **A plan is not a slide.** If a module reads like a bullet list from a deck,
  it has not been thought about visually — say what the reader should SEE.

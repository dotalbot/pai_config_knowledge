1. **Productise The Technical Gap Immediately**

Create a fixed-scope offer that bridges adoption and architecture:

`Copilot Safe-to-Scale Architecture Assessment`

It should diagnose whether a client is technically ready for Copilot at enterprise scale across:

- Microsoft 365 tenant architecture
- Purview, sensitivity labels, retention, DLP
- SharePoint and Teams sprawl
- identity and conditional access
- data oversharing risk
- Copilot Studio extensibility readiness
- Power Platform governance
- auditability and regulatory exposure

Output should not be a generic report. It should produce:

- executive risk map
- target architecture
- remediation roadmap
- backlog of funded implementation work
- adoption impact narrative for change teams

Why it matters: Inform already has trust with clients on adoption. Dom turns that trust into technical remediation revenue.

Wrong move: trying to sell “architecture consulting” as a vague new capability. Nobody buys that. They buy reduced Copilot risk and a path to deployment.

2. **Turn Existing Adoption Clients Into Architecture Expansion Accounts**

Dom’s fastest revenue will not come from net-new logos. It will come from existing clients who already heard Inform say:

“You need better governance before Copilot.”

He should review every current and recent Copilot / M365 adoption engagement and classify accounts into:

- ready to expand into technical assessment
- politically blocked
- technically immature but budgetable
- strong case-study candidate
- not worth pursuing

Then create account-specific expansion plays.

For example:

- NHS: information governance, records, clinical safety, oversharing
- MoD / Home Office: security, classification, auditability, sovereign constraints
- councils: Teams/SharePoint sprawl, records retention, citizen data exposure
- pharma: regulated knowledge management, IP protection, R&D data boundaries

Why it matters: Inform’s change consultants are probably sitting on latent technical demand but lack the vocabulary to convert it.

Wrong move: asking sales to “go sell architecture.” Instead, arm adoption consultants with trigger phrases, qualification questions, and escalation paths to Dom.

3. **Build A Microsoft AI Governance Reference Architecture**

Dom should create Inform’s own opinionated architecture for safe Microsoft AI adoption.

Not a 60-page whitepaper. A practical market artifact:

`The Inform Microsoft AI Governance Architecture`

It should show how the stack fits together:

- Entra ID
- Microsoft 365
- Purview
- Defender
- SharePoint Advanced Management
- Teams governance
- Power Platform CoE
- Copilot Studio
- Azure AI where relevant
- audit, risk, compliance, adoption telemetry

This becomes the centrepiece for sales, delivery, webinars, partner conversations, and internal enablement.

Why it matters: Inform needs to stop sounding like a change consultancy that has hired an architect. It needs to sound like the UK specialist in safe Microsoft AI transformation.

Wrong move: building bespoke architecture from scratch for every client. That does not compound. A reference architecture does.

4. **Create A Technical Delivery Spine Inside The Firm**

Dom should not become the heroic bottleneck.

In 180 days, he needs a lightweight architecture operating model:

- architecture review board for client work
- reusable discovery templates
- standard Copilot readiness model
- technical risk taxonomy
- design patterns
- escalation routes from consultants to architecture
- “red flag” checklist for adoption teams
- solution assurance gates before client recommendations

Then train 8-12 senior consultants to spot technical architecture risk, even if they cannot solve it.

Why it matters: the whole company must become architecture-aware. Dom alone cannot scale revenue. But Dom plus 90 consultants who can identify architecture-led expansion opportunities can.

Wrong move: hiring a small architecture team too early without embedding architecture into Inform’s existing delivery engine.

5. **Own The Board-Level Copilot Risk Conversation**

Inform’s current positioning is likely too soft: adoption, readiness, change, people.

Dom should help reposition Copilot as a board-level risk and value issue:

“Copilot does not create governance problems. It reveals the ones you already have.”

That message is commercially powerful.

He should build a founder-led executive briefing package for CIOs, CDOs, CISOs, HRDs, and transformation leaders:

- what Copilot exposes
- why adoption alone is insufficient
- where data leakage actually happens
- why governance must be architectural, not policy-only
- what a safe deployment path looks like
- how to move from pilots to scale

Why it matters: this gets Inform out of low-margin training/adoption conversations and into funded transformation, risk, and platform-modernisation budgets.

Wrong move: marketing Copilot as productivity uplift first. Everyone is doing that. The sharper wedge is controlled acceleration: move faster because the architecture is safe.

**The Contrarian View**

Most firms would do this:

- announce a new AI architecture practice
- hire architects
- make generic capability decks
- chase net-new enterprise clients
- talk about Copilot productivity
- run pilots and workshops
- produce readiness reports

That is wrong.

Inform’s unfair advantage is not architecture yet. It is trusted access to organisations already struggling with Microsoft adoption. Dom’s job is to convert adoption trust into technical authority, then technical authority into remediation and scale programmes.

The best 180-day strategy is:

- productise the gap
- mine existing accounts
- create a reusable reference architecture
- make the whole firm architecture-aware
- reposition Copilot as a governance and risk architecture problem

If Dom does that, Inform stops being “the people side of Copilot” and becomes “the firm that makes Microsoft AI safe enough to scale.”

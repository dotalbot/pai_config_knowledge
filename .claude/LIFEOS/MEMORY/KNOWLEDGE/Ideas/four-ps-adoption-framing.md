# The 4 Ps — Dom's framework for framing an adoption

**People · Process · Policy · Technology/Product**

**This is Dom's own framework**, named by him on 2026-08-21 and confirmed as his on
2026-08-22: *"you can record them as my framework, you will hear of them many times
in the future."* Expect it to recur in his work and language.

## The claim

Cynefin says to probe when you are in the Complex domain. It never says **across
what**. That gap is where the technology-first instinct creeps back in — teams
"probe" by piloting a product and call it done.

**A probe that touches only Technology/Product is not a probe. It is a demo.**

## The four

- **People** — skills, will, trust, roles. Whose job changes, and who loses status
  by adopting this?
- **Process** — does the way work actually gets done change, or does the tool get
  bolted onto an unchanged process and quietly abandoned?
- **Policy** — governance, data classification, DLP, retention, compliance.
  Usually discovered last and usually the blocker.
- **Technology/Product** — the tool. The only one most pilots test, and the least
  likely reason adoption fails.

**It is FOUR Ps, not five.** Technology/Product is a single P. Dom writes it as
"technology" sometimes and "product" other times; they mean the same thing to him
(confirmed 2026-08-22). Do not split it, and do not correct his wording when he
uses either term alone.

## Why it matters to him

Most AI pilots probe the product and discover far too late that the people would
not use it, the process never changed, or policy forbade the data it needed. The
pilot succeeded and the adoption failed — the signature of the wrong probe.

Pairs with his related position that AI adoption is Complex, not *just*
Complicated: the domain has to be framed at initiation, before an approach is
chosen, or you default to the familiar Complicated response.

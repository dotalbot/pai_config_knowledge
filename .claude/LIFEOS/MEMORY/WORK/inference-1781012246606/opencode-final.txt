2. **Salary Anchor**

Dom should name **£125,000 base** first.

Why:

- Posted range tops at **£105k**, but that is for the JD, not the actual role Dom is shaping.
- Dom is not just filling Senior Solutions Architect. He is potentially becoming their **first architecture function**, **first engineering-capable AI practitioner**, and **internal Frontier Firm transformation lead**.
- UK top-end Senior/Principal AI/Copilot architecture market is **£100k-£125k**.
- Fractional equivalent is **£96k-£180k/year**, and Dom would be giving them full-time access.
- Microsoft direct pays **£128k-£174k**, so **£125k** is still founder-friendly compared with enterprise tech compensation.

Recommended phrasing:

> “The posted range makes sense for a Senior Solutions Architect delivery role. The role we’re discussing sounds broader: first architect, internal AI transformation, client-facing AI/Copilot architecture, and helping build technical capability around Microsoft partnership. For that scope, I’d anchor the base at **£125k**.”

Target outcome:

- Strong outcome: **£120k base**
- Acceptable outcome: **£110k-£115k base plus structured upside**
- Do not accept plain **£105k** unless there is a written step-up, bonus, and equity/profit-share mechanism.

3. **Equity Conversation**

Best mechanism: **phantom equity or profit-share**, not ordinary equity as the first ask.

Why:

- A 54-person founder-led UK consultancy with no PE may be emotionally protective of actual equity.
- EMI options are possible, but only make sense if they already have or are willing to create a share option scheme.
- Phantom equity gives Dom upside tied to value creation without forcing founders to dilute immediately.
- Profit-share is simplest operationally but weaker than phantom equity because it can be affected by accounting decisions.

Recommended order of ask:

1. **Phantom equity: 1.0%-1.5% of company value growth above today’s baseline**
2. If they resist: **AI/Copilot practice profit-share: 5%-8% of gross profit from net-new AI/Copilot architecture revenue Dom originates or materially enables**
3. If they already have EMI: **EMI options worth 0.5%-1.0% fully diluted**, vesting over 4 years with a 1-year cliff

Exact ask:

> “I’m not trying to create a founder-level equity issue. But if I’m helping create a new AI architecture capability, improve Microsoft partner status, and open higher-value client work, I’d like some upside linked to that value creation. The cleanest version could be phantom equity: **1% of company value growth above today’s baseline**, vesting over four years. If actual equity is too heavy administratively, I’d also be open to a profit-share tied specifically to AI/Copilot revenue.”

Numbers:

- Ask: **1.0% phantom equity**
- Stretch: **1.5% phantom equity**
- Fallback: **5%-8% profit-share on AI/Copilot gross profit**
- EMI option ask: **0.5%-1.0%**

Do not ask for more than **1.5%** initially. Above that starts sounding like co-founder economics.

5. **One Red Flag To Address Head-On**

The red flag: **They may want Dom’s strategic architecture, AI transformation, Microsoft partnership uplift, mentoring, and hands-on delivery, but pay him as a standard Senior Solutions Architect.**

Dom should say this directly but diplomatically:

> “The only thing I’d want to avoid is mismatched expectations. If this is primarily a Senior Solutions Architect delivery role, the posted range makes sense. If the real opportunity is to help The Inform Team build its first architecture capability, shape the AI/Copilot offer, support Microsoft partner progression, mentor consultants, and create new client value, then I’d suggest we structure the role, title, and upside around that broader remit.”

That frames the negotiation correctly: not “pay me more because I want more,” but “define the real scope, then match the package to it.”

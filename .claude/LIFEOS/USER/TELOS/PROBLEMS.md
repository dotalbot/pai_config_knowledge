# Problems Being Solved

> External problems in the world you're working to address.

- **P0:** AI hype over substance — businesses are being sold promise, not delivery. Vendors win; organisations are left holding shelfware.
- **P1:** Organisations lack the skills to evaluate what they're buying — no internal benchmark, no way to call BS on the pitch.
  **Status 2026-08-24: ACTIVE, and the framing has moved.**

  *Fed in during the P1 session, 2026-08-24:*

  The work is **building frameworks that give an organisation a pragmatic path
  to grow AI maturity, with value landing along the way rather than at the end
  of the climb.** Applies to customers and internally at Inform, which makes
  Inform both practitioner and case study.

  **This is a reframe, not an update.** June's P1 was defensive — organisations
  cannot tell if they are being sold snake oil, no benchmark, no way to call BS.
  What Dom is actually building is constructive: not "how do you spot a bad
  pitch" but *"what is the sequence of moves that takes you from here to
  capable, paying for itself as it goes."* Evaluation capability turns out to be
  a by-product of maturity, not the thing itself. An organisation that knows
  what good looks like at its own next step can evaluate a pitch; one that
  cannot is defenceless regardless of how many checklists it holds.

  **It is deliberately both** a framework customers use *and* Inform's internal
  delivery method. Confirmed when asked directly. Those normally diverge —
  different artefact, audience, and definition of done — so holding both is a
  choice with consequences worth watching rather than an unresolved ambiguity.

  **The load-bearing constraint is "value along the way."** It rules out the
  standard maturity-model shape, where an organisation invests through several
  levels before anything pays back. That is the same objection Dom's TELOS makes
  everywhere else: M0 wants working products not hype, P0 names shelfware as the
  failure, and his BELIEFS require small slices of value delivered as soon as
  possible. P1 is those beliefs applied to somebody else's transformation.

  ⚠️ *Still to feed in: what exists so far (documents? a draft? just thinking?),
  who inside Inform is involved, whether there is a customer waiting on it, and
  what the first shippable increment looks like.*
- **P2:** Enterprise architects are being used as rubber stamps, not thought leaders — their leverage is wasted on sign-off ceremonies instead of shaping strategy.
  **Status 2026-08-24: REFRAMED.** Less of a live problem in the new role. The
  question has shifted from *"architects are sidelined"* to *"how do I influence
  this organisation from the position I now hold."* That is a different problem
  with different moves — it is about internal influence, not professional
  standing. ⚠️ *Also under-specified: needs a feed-in session.*

## Notes

- A current state that's objectively worse than it could be
- A plausible path toward improvement

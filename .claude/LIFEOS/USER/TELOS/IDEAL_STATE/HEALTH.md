# Ideal State — Health

> What "sorted" looks like physically. Targets, not current state.

## Body Composition

- **Target weight:** 85kg
- **Build:** Slightly muscular, fit — not bulk, just solid

## Sleep

- **Target:** 8 hours — **holding.** Sleep is not the problem; it is protected
  and valued.
- **Quality:** Wake up refreshed, not dragging

## Fitness

- **Goal:** Consistency over performance — regular movement that feels good
- **Benchmark:** Not out of breath after moderate exercise
- **Mobility:** Good range of motion, no chronic stiffness

## Recovery — the actual constraint (added 2026-08-24)

**This is the real problem, and it is not a motivation problem.** Exertion is
followed by exhaustion lasting *days*, scaling with the effort applied. It is
long-standing, not new. Good sleep does not resolve it.

- **What this rules out:** conventional "just be consistent" fitness advice.
  A programme that assumes next-day recovery will fail on contact, and failing
  it repeatedly is demoralising in a way that has nothing to do with discipline.
- **Ideal state:** exert on a Saturday, functional on a Sunday. Effort has a
  proportionate, bounded recovery cost instead of an open-ended one.
- **Why it matters now:** this is the thing standing between current state and
  every other target on this page, and it gets harder to fix with age.

> [!warning] Not diagnosed, and worth being
> Post-exertional exhaustion lasting days is a specific, recognised pattern with
> several possible causes, some straightforwardly testable. This file records
> what Dom experiences; it is not a self-diagnosis and I am not qualified to
> make one. **Open action: raise with a GP, and ask specifically about
> post-exertional fatigue rather than describing it as "being unfit."**

## North Star

Nimble and mobile. Not sluggish. Not stiff. Move through the day without the
body getting in the way — **and be able to use the body without paying for it
for three days afterwards.**
